Section 3: Executive Summary¶

This project applies unstructured data analytics to Yelp reviews of music venues to uncover what drives customer satisfaction and dissatisfaction in live entertainment experiences. Using 13,519 reviews from 1,104 music venues in calendar year 2019 (pre-COVID) across the U.S. and Canada, we will perform sentiment analysis and topic modeling to identify key themes that differentiate highly rated venues from poorly rated ones. The insights will help venue operators, booking agents, and event promoters understand what aspects of the customer experience matter most—e.g., sound quality and atmosphere, crowd management, staff/security interactions, and pricing/value—enabling data-driven improvements to operations and promotion strategy.

# Define Venue Category Buckets
cat_keywords = {
    'Bar-oriented': ['Bars', 'Dive Bars', 'Sports Bars', 'Pubs', 'Cocktail Bars', 'Beer Bar', 'Wine Bars', 'Hookah Bars', 'Lounges', 'Breweries'],
    'Restaurant-hybrid': ['Restaurants'],
    'Event Space': ['Venues & Event Spaces', 'Event Planning & Services', 'Party & Event Planning', 'Performing Arts'],
    'Dance/Club': ['Dance Clubs', 'Karaoke']
}

def get_venue_types(categories_str):
    types = []
    if not isinstance(categories_str, str):
        return types
    for cat_name, keywords in cat_keywords.items():
        for keyword in keywords:
            if keyword in categories_str:
                types.append(cat_name)
                # Keep checking other buckets as it can overlap
                break 
    return types

# Apply to businesses

Section 6: Data Evaluation¶

a. How the selected data are suitable for answering the business questions: The data provides a direct link between customer sentiment (reviews) and business categories ("Music Venues"), allowing us to isolate the target industry. The richness of the text data enables fine-grained analysis of specific drivers like sound quality and atmosphere, which numeric ratings alone cannot capture.

b. Whether the sample size is appropriate and why: With over 13,000 reviews and 1,100 businesses, the sample size is statistically robust for identifying trends and training topic models. It covers a wide geographic area and a full calendar year, mitigating seasonal biases and small-sample noise.

c. Whether the data could be biased in certain dimensions: The data is likely biased towards urban centers (top cities) and users who are motivated to write reviews (often very positive or very negative experiences). Furthermore, 2019 data might reflect a specific economic climate that has since changed, though it serves as a good pre-pandemic baseline.

d. Potential challenges to process the data: The unstructured text requires significant cleaning (handling slang, typos, emojis). Additionally, distinguishing between the "venue" experience and the "performer" experience in music venue reviews will be a semantic challenge for sentiment and topic modeling algorithms.

# 1. Apply VADER Sentiment Analysis
sid = SentimentIntensityAnalyzer()

def get_sentiment_class(score):
    if score >= 0.05:
        return 'Positive'
    elif score <= -0.05:
        return 'Negative'
    else:
        return 'Neutral'

# Calculate scores
review_df['vader_compound'] = review_df['text'].apply(lambda x: sid.polarity_scores(str(x))['compound'])

# 2. Classify reviews

Brief Markdown Interpretation of Sentiment Findings¶

Overall Alignment: As expected, the VADER compound score correlates strongly with star ratings, with 5-star reviews appearing overwhelmingly positive and 1-star reviews overwhelmingly negative.

Mismatches ("Talent Trap" Hypothesis): The mismatched reviews reveal a notable paradox. We identified over 1,500 reviews with low star ratings (1–2) but positive sentiment scores. These reviews frequently use positive language to describe the talent (e.g., "excited," "favorite artist," "great music") while assigning poor ratings due to operational issues such as security, lines, and crowd management. The resulting 11:1 ratio compared to the reverse case (high star / negative sentiment) suggests that operational failures, rather than talent quality, may be a primary driver of customer dissatisfaction. However, this pattern could also partly reflect VADER's known limitations with sarcasm, irony, and mixed-sentiment text — a distinction we will investigate further with supervised methods in the next phase.

Venue Types: Segmentation by venue type reveals a clear operational hierarchy. Event Spaces and Performing Arts venues show higher median sentiment, likely reflecting more structured operations (seated formats, ticketing, professional staff). In contrast, Dance Clubs exhibit lower sentiment and greater variance, indicating that high-friction environments (crowding, security checks, entry delays) are dominant contributors to negative sentiment.

Seasonality: Monthly trends for 2019 are relatively stable, without pronounced volatility. This indicates that customer sentiment in live-music venues is driven primarily by operational consistency rather than seasonality (unlike retail or travel). This stability further supports using 2019 as a reliable pre-COVID baseline for defining standard venue performance benchmarks.

Section 8: Proposed Solution¶

To address our core research question—Does operational friction drive negative sentiment more than talent quality?—we propose a multi-stage unstructured data analytics pipeline.

1. Topic Modeling (Unsupervised Learning — Required)

Method: Apply Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) and Non-Negative Matrix Factorization (NMF) to the subset of negative reviews (1–2 stars).

Purpose: To identify latent thematic structures underlying customer complaints.

Business Value: We hypothesize that negative topics will cluster around operational friction (e.g., security, lines, drinks, bouncers) rather than talent quality (e.g., band, song, sound). Validation of this pattern would demonstrate that venues primarily lose customers due to operational failures at the point of service, not performance quality on stage.

2. Sentiment Classification (Supervised Learning)

Method: Build a Logistic Regression classifier using TF-IDF vectorization with n-grams to predict star ratings from review text.

Purpose: To establish a baseline model identifying words that are predictive of review polarity.

Business Value: Inspection of model coefficients will reveal which terms are most strongly associated with 1-star ratings (e.g., "rude," "wait," "manager") versus 5-star ratings (e.g., "amazing," "atmosphere"), translating unstructured text into actionable operational signals.

3. Comparative and Temporal Segmentation

Method: Segment topic distributions and sentiment scores by venue type (e.g., clubs versus concert halls) and by month.

Purpose: To control for contextual bias in sentiment expression.

Business Value: This segmentation supports tailored managerial recommendations, such as expecting security to dominate negative sentiment in clubs, while seating or acoustics may emerge as key drivers of dissatisfaction in performing arts venues.

4. Optional Extensions (if time and interpretability justify)

  • Aspect-Based Sentiment Analysis: Apply VADER in conjunction with dependency parsing or noun-phrase extraction to disentangle sentiment directed at staff/operations versus music/talent, enabling direct quantification of the "Talent Trap" hypothesis.
  • VADER vs. Supervised Comparison: Evaluate where VADER misclassifies relative to the logistic regression baseline to characterize lexicon-based limitations in this domain.
  • BERTopic: Explore as a robustness check on LDA/NMF topic coherence.
